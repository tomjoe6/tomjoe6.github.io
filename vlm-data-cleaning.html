技术报告漫读：工业界怎么洗数据

我们洗数据是全程 VLM 的，成本也完全付得起；直到把六份前沿技术报告放在一起精读，才发现它们的过滤段没有一家用 VLM。这是为什么呢？

前段时间我们洗了一批视频数据，从去重、初筛、重筛、富矿挖掘、事件窗定位，到 caption 重写与泄漏校验，八道工序中除了第一道 ffprobe，其余全部由 VLM 出判决。流程跑下来效果不错，账单也在可接受范围内，于是笔者很自然地形成一个想法：数据清洗本质上是个判断问题，模型越强判断越准，拿 VLM 洗数据理应就是更先进的做法。

后来闲下来，笔者把手上能找到的前沿视频生成技术报告都翻了出来：MovieGen、Seedance 1.0、HunyuanVideo 1.5、Open-Sora 2.0，加上两篇专门讲数据集的 Koala-36M 和 Tiger200K，把数据部分放在一起精读了一遍。结果发现一个有点出乎意料的事实：这些管线的过滤段，没有一家用 VLM。上一篇《SOTA视频模型的物理表现还有提升空间》看的是评测端，这篇往上游走一步，看数据端。

几个数字值得单独说说。最便宜的一道工序恰恰不是 VLM：第 0 道用 ffprobe 在 CPU 上就把 15.2 万条砍到 10.4 万，成本约等于零。真到了要省钱的时候，我们自己也毫不犹豫把最便宜的工具放在最前面，这个事实后面还会用到。

八道工序合计约 257 GPU·小时，平均每条源视频约 6 卡秒。这个数字乍看不大，但请注意其中真正花在 caption 上的只有第 6、7 道的 12.9 GPU·小时，不到总账的 5%；剩下 95% 的算力，全都花在判断"要不要这条数据"上。15.2 万条进去，最后入档 1.2 万条，总留存 8%。换句话说，VLM 管线的成本大头在过滤而不在 caption，这一点后面也会用到。

可以看到，VLM 只出现在三个位置：caption、后训练的小规模判断，以及 RL 阶段的奖励模型。值得注意的是，这三个位置其实是同一种身份：captioner 放大人类写描述的方式，奖励模型放大人类的偏好判断，都不是独立裁判。而在过滤段，也就是镜头切分、质量过滤、美学打分、运动过滤、去重这一段，清一色是 PySceneDetect、PaddleOCR、Laplacian 方差、VMAF、光流、CLIP+MLP 这些工具和小模型。我们对照了 2026 年的 Seedance 2.0 报告，数据引擎与 1.0 同构，过滤段依旧没有 VLM——两代模型，同一个模式。

六层漏斗

把六份报告叠在一起，工业界洗数据的流水线大致是六层。第一层是切分：PySceneDetect、TransNetV2、FFmpeg 的场景分数把原始视频切成 2 到 16 秒的单镜头 clip，转场和特效由专门的小分类器清掉。Tiger200K 给过一个细节：阈值式的 PySceneDetect 会漏叠化转场，所以他们换了学习式的 TransNetV2。这一层没有理解，只有逐帧定位。

第二层是一批便宜的量具。水印和字幕交给 PaddleOCR 和专用检测器，裁出安全区，裁完保留面积不足就整条丢弃；清晰度用 Laplacian 方差和 VMAF；美学是 CLIP+MLP 小回归器；运动用光流和 VMAF motion，太静和太抖都删。全是冻结的小模型，毫秒级一条。2026 年的 Seedance 2.0 报告里，这一层还是同样的配置。

第三层是去重与配比，操作对象从单条视频换成了分布。clip 用视频表征模型编码，聚类之后每个近重复簇只留质量分最高的那条；再按主体、场景、动作、风格数分布，头部下采样、尾部定向补采（Seedance），MovieGen 则用聚类反比采样加一套 600 个动词的词表做概念平衡。

第四层 caption，VLM 在这里进场，但没有一家裸用通用 VLM：Seedance 在 Tarsier2 上专训了双语 captioner，MovieGen 微调 LLaMA3-Video，HunyuanVideo 用 OPA-DPO 后训练来平衡描述密度与幻觉，输出的都是 80 到 200 词的密集结构化描述；Seedance 甚至单独训了一个 prompt 改写器，保证用户输入和 caption 同分布。

第五层是数据金字塔。同一批语料按质量分出梯度：Open-Sora 2.0 是 7000 万的低分辨率池喂出 500 万的高分辨率精选，HunyuanVideo 从 1000 万小时原始视频筛出 8 亿 clip，逐级收紧，贵的高分辨率训练只吃塔尖那一层。

第六层是后训练和 RLHF，筛法在这里彻底变了：不再是过滤大池子，而是人工构造一个小数据集。prompt 池人工验证（HunyuanVideo 的 I2V RL 用了 100 多个类目），候选由不同 checkpoint 对同一 prompt 滚出，人来成对标注（Meta 用 GSB，Seedance 按维度选 best/worst，HunyuanVideo 标了约 1 万对 DPO），最后把这 1 万对人类偏好蒸馏成 VLM 奖励模型，供在线 RL 批量打分。8 亿 clip 对 1 万对标注，差五个数量级，越靠近塔尖，数据越小、越贵、也越真。顺带一提，HunyuanVideo 自己承认现有奖励模型分不清细粒度运动的好坏，所以才先做离线 DPO 再上在线 RL：VLM 裁判也是蒸馏来的，人分不清的它同样分不清。

既然我们用 VLM 洗得好好的，为什么大厂不在过滤段也用它？

贵多少

最直接的解释当然是贵。这个说法大差不差，但它属于"万能模版"式的回答，什么问题都可以拿"贵"来搪塞，所以我们不妨把账认真算一遍。有意思的是，六份报告没有一个披露数据处理本身的算力开销：HunyuanVideo 只给了"1000 万小时原始视频 → 8 亿 clip"的比例，Open-Sora 只给了 20 万美元的模型训练成本。所以这笔账只能拿自己的实测去外推。

每条 6 卡秒，乘以 8 亿 clip，约等于 135 万 A800·小时，折合 150 卡年。什么概念呢？一千张 A800 不吃不喝跑五十多天，才筛完一遍；而数据引擎的常态是每个模型版本迭代都重筛。另一边，CLIP+MLP 美学回归器是毫秒级一帧，前后差了三个数量级。所以在预训练的规模上，全程 VLM 不是贵一点，是根本跑不动。

可能有读者觉得：那不就是纯粹的贵而已，等 VLM 推理降价了自然就换过去了。咋看之下有道理，但笔者认为不成立，问题出在过滤器这个角色的特殊性上。

量具与裁判

过滤器和 captioner 不一样：captioner 的输出直接进训练，好坏由下游模型消化；过滤器定义的是整个语料的分布，所以它首先得是一件量具。而量具的第一美德是稳定，不是聪明。

CLIP+MLP 美学回归器是冻结的：同一个模型、同一个分数语义，跑十遍结果一致，失败模式已知，分数分布可以画出来审计。VLM 则是生成器：prompt 换个措辞、底座升个版本、甚至同一批数据重跑一遍，分数的含义都可能漂移。同一条视频这个月 4.2 分、下个月 4.8 分，对 caption 来说无所谓，对过滤器来说是灾难，因为整个语料的筛选标准跟着变了。

这里还有一层更微妙的关系。美学回归器是什么？是在人类美学评分数据上蒸馏出来的 CLIP+MLP；VLM 是什么？同样是人类判断的蒸馏。两者本是同源，所以大厂在过滤段做的选择，其实不是要哪份知识，而是要哪种容器：冻结的标量，还是会漂移的语言。

写到这里，笔者回头看了眼自己的管线，发现一个有点好笑的事实。我们为了把 VLM 用便宜，把 12–16 段拼成一张蒙太奇、一个请求塞 5 条视频 × 3 个 chunk，这些技巧的本质，就是逼 VLM 一次前向吐出尽可能多的标量判决，把它往编码器的用法上推。换句话说，在成本压力下，VLM 重管线会自发长出小模型管线的行为模式。我们和大厂，其实是在从两端做同一件事。

代理塔

再往深一层想，就碰到"什么是好数据"本身了。理想的定义其实很清楚：U(x) 等于用 x 训练之后模型的期望提升。麻烦的是这个量没法计算，唯一的测法是真的拿去训练再评测。所以整条数据管线本质上是一座代理塔：塔尖是真训真测，塔下面的一切，元数据、质量分、美学分、embedding 几何、VLM 判断、甚至人工标注，都是对塔尖的代理，越往上越准也越贵。漏斗的规则是便宜的看全部、贵的只看幸存者，而这个排序由单条成本决定，并不由哪个信号最接近 U 决定。

用这个视角回看，一些原来散落的现象就都能解释了。Tiger200K 为什么敢不做任何训练实验就发数据集？因为大家都把代理当成了本体，默认过滤了就是好的。学界论文为什么清一色 VLM 洗数据？算力有限，级联退化成一层，那就只留最全能的那层。HunyuanVideo 为什么花力气用 RL 专训 captioner，而不是直接用 Qwen？因为 caption 是离塔尖最近、又能低成本对齐的环节。各家为什么都把数据飞轮当终局？因为飞轮把塔尖变成了日常操作。

这也意味着"好数据"并没有属性式的定义，只有阶段式的：同一条 clip，在 256p 预训练是好数据，到 768p 精调就是坏数据。"好"不是视频自己的属性，而是视频、训练阶段和目标三者之间的关系。

拓展思考

最后说一个可能被未来打脸的预测。当时实测的稳定吞吐，7B 大约 35–40 条/分/卡，换成大四倍的 30B 大约 30 条/分/卡，模型大了四倍，单卡吞吐基本没掉，可见 serving 效率的进步是实打实的。如果 VLM 推理成本再降两个数量级，量具论会松动，过滤段逐步换成 VLM judge 是大概率事件，AVVA 这类工作和大厂 RL 里的 VLM 奖励模型已经是先声。到那时候回头看，CLIP+MLP 美学回归器大概就像我们今天看像素阈值过滤一样原始。

两种管线正在相向而行：我们在学着把 VLM 用成编码器，大厂在把手工过滤器换成 learned judge。至于成本真正崩塌之后，"好数据"会等来一个认识论上的定义，还是只换一个更贵的共识，笔者暂时还没有想清楚。

文章小结

这篇文章用我们自己的一份账单和六份技术报告，把工业界的洗数据管线拆成了六层，并回答了一个问题：为什么过滤段没有 VLM。直接原因是贵；更本质的原因是身份，VLM 在这条管线里始终以人类判断放大器的身份出现，而过滤器要的是量具的稳定，两者要的从来不是同一种东西。

Tech Report Digest: How the Industry Cleans Data

We cleaned our data entirely with VLMs, and the bill was perfectly affordable. Then I put six frontier technical reports side by side and found that not one of them uses a VLM in the filtering stage. Why?

A while ago we cleaned a batch of video data. Deduplication, first pass, tiered admission, strong-prompt re-screening, dense mining, event-window localization, caption rewriting, leakage checks — of the eight stages, all but the first ffprobe pass were judged by VLMs. The results were good and the bill was acceptable, so I naturally settled into a belief: data cleaning is at bottom a judgment problem, stronger models judge better, so cleaning with VLMs is simply the more advanced approach.

Later, with some time to spare, I dug out every frontier video generation technical report I could find — MovieGen, Seedance 1.0, HunyuanVideo 1.5, Open-Sora 2.0, plus two dataset papers, Koala-36M and Tiger200K — and read their data sections side by side. The finding was a bit unexpected: none of these pipelines uses a VLM in the filtering stage. My previous post looked at the evaluation end; this one takes a step upstream, to the data end.

Before going further, let me lay out our bill, since every extrapolation later builds on it:

A few numbers deserve a separate look. The cheapest stage is precisely the one that is not a VLM: stage 0 uses ffprobe on CPU to cut 152k down to 104k at essentially zero cost. When money actually matters, we too reach for the cheapest tool first, without hesitation — a fact that will come in handy later.

The eight stages total about 257 GPU·hours, roughly 6 card-seconds per source video. That sounds small, but note that what actually went into captioning — stages 6 and 7 — is only 12.9 GPU·hours, under 5% of the bill; the other 95% went into deciding whether to keep each item. 152k in, 12k filed, an overall retention of 8%. In other words, the cost center of a VLM pipeline is filtering, not captioning — and this fact will also come in handy later.

VLMs show up in exactly three places: captioning, small-scale post-training judgments, and reward models in RL. These three are really one identity: the captioner amplifies how humans describe videos, the reward model amplifies human preference — neither is an independent judge. In the filtering stage — shot segmentation, quality filtering, aesthetic scoring, motion filtering, dedup — it is PySceneDetect, PaddleOCR, Laplacian variance, VMAF, optical flow, and CLIP+MLP all the way down. We also checked the 2026 Seedance 2.0 report: its data engine is isomorphic to 1.0, and its filtering stage is still VLM-free — two generations, the same pattern.

The six-layer funnel

Stacking the six reports together, the industry's cleaning pipeline has roughly six layers. The first is segmentation: PySceneDetect, TransNetV2, and FFmpeg scene scores cut raw footage into single-shot clips of 2 to 16 seconds, and dedicated small classifiers remove transitions and effects. Tiger200K offers a detail: threshold-based PySceneDetect misses cross-dissolves, so they switched to the learned TransNetV2. There is no understanding in this layer, only per-frame localization.

The second layer is a batch of cheap instruments. Watermarks and subtitles go to PaddleOCR and dedicated detectors that crop a safe zone, discarding the clip if too little frame remains; sharpness is Laplacian variance and VMAF; aesthetics is a small CLIP+MLP regressor; motion is optical flow and VMAF motion, with both the too-still and the too-shaky removed. All frozen small models, milliseconds per item. In the 2026 Seedance 2.0 report, this layer runs the same configuration.

The third layer is dedup and rebalancing, where the object of operation shifts from individual clips to distributions. Clips are embedded by a video representation model and clustered; each near-duplicate cluster keeps only its highest-quality member. Then the distribution is counted over subject, scene, action, and style — heads are downsampled, tails get targeted acquisition (Seedance), and MovieGen combines inverse-proportional cluster sampling with a taxonomy of 600 human verbs for concept balancing.

The fourth layer is captioning, where VLMs enter — but none uses a stock general VLM: Seedance trained a bilingual captioner on Tarsier2, MovieGen fine-tuned LLaMA3-Video, HunyuanVideo used OPA-DPO post-training to balance description density against hallucination; the outputs are dense structured descriptions of 80 to 200 words. Seedance even trained a separate prompt rewriter so that user inputs stay in the same distribution as captions.

The fifth layer is the data pyramid. The same corpus is graded by quality: Open-Sora 2.0 feeds a 70M low-resolution pool down into a 5M high-resolution selection, and HunyuanVideo filters 10M raw hours into 800M clips, tightening level by level; expensive high-resolution training only eats from the top.

The sixth layer is post-training and RLHF, where the method changes completely: no longer filtering a big corpus, but hand-constructing a tiny dataset. The prompt pool is human-verified (HunyuanVideo used 100-plus categories for I2V RL), candidates are rolled out from different checkpoints on the same prompt, humans label pairwise preferences (Meta used GSB, Seedance picked best/worst per dimension, HunyuanVideo annotated about 10k DPO pairs), and finally those 10k human preferences are distilled into a VLM reward model that scores rollouts in bulk during online RL. 800M clips versus 10k annotations — five orders of magnitude apart. The closer to the top of the tower, the smaller, pricier, and truer the data. Incidentally, HunyuanVideo itself admits that existing reward models cannot tell apart fine-grained motion quality, which is why they run offline DPO before online RL: a VLM judge is also distilled — whatever humans cannot distinguish, it cannot either.

Since VLMs worked fine for us, why don't frontier labs use them in the filtering stage too?

How expensive

The immediate explanation is cost. That answer is roughly right, but it is a template answer — almost anything can be waved off as "too expensive" — so let's run the numbers properly. Interestingly, none of the six reports discloses the compute cost of data processing itself: HunyuanVideo gives only the ratio "10M raw hours → 800M clips", Open-Sora only a $200k training budget. So the only way to do the accounting is to extrapolate from our own measurements.

Six card-seconds per item, times 800M clips, is about 1.35M A800·hours, or 150 card-years. Concretely: a thousand A800s grinding for over fifty days straight buys exactly one pass, and data engines routinely re-run the funnel for every model iteration. On the other side, a CLIP+MLP aesthetic regressor runs at milliseconds per frame — a three-orders-of-magnitude gap. At pre-training scale, an all-VLM pipeline is not somewhat expensive; it simply does not run.

Some readers may think: fine, it is purely a cost issue, and once VLM inference gets cheaper everyone will switch. That sounds right at first glance, but I don't think it holds, and the reason lies in what a filter is.

Instruments and judges

A filter is not a captioner. A captioner's output goes straight into training and is digested by the downstream model; a filter defines the distribution of the entire corpus, so first and foremost it is a measuring instrument. The first virtue of an instrument is stability, not intelligence.

A CLIP+MLP aesthetic regressor is frozen: same model, same score semantics, identical results across ten runs, known failure modes, and a score distribution you can plot and audit. A VLM is a generator: reword the prompt, upgrade the base model, or simply re-run the same batch, and the meaning of the score can drift. The same video scoring 4.2 this month and 4.8 next month is harmless for a caption and disastrous for a filter, because the selection criterion of the whole corpus just moved.

There is a subtler relationship here. What is an aesthetic regressor? A CLIP+MLP distilled from human aesthetic ratings. What is a VLM? Also a distillation of human judgment. The two share a source, so the choice made in the filtering stage was never about which knowledge to use, but about which container: a frozen scalar, or drifting language.

At this point I looked back at our own pipeline and found something almost funny. To make VLMs cheap, we tile 12–16 segments into one montage image and pack 5 videos × 3 chunks into a single request. The essence of these tricks is to force the VLM to emit as many scalar verdicts as possible per forward pass — pushing it toward encoder-style usage. Under cost pressure, a VLM-heavy pipeline spontaneously grows the behavioral patterns of a small-model pipeline. We and the frontier labs are doing the same thing from opposite ends.

A tower of proxies

One level deeper, we bump into "what is good data" itself. The ideal definition is clear enough: U(x) equals the expected improvement of the model after training on x. The trouble is that this quantity cannot be computed; the only way to measure it is to actually train and evaluate. So the entire pipeline is a tower of proxies: the top is train-and-measure, and everything below — metadata, quality scores, aesthetic scores, embedding geometry, VLM judgments, even human annotation — is a proxy for the top, each level more accurate and more expensive. The funnel's rule is that the cheap look at everything and the expensive look only at survivors, and this ordering is decided by per-item cost, not by which signal is closest to U.

Seen through this lens, several scattered facts line up. Why could Tiger200K publish a dataset without a single training experiment? Because everyone mistakes the proxy for the thing itself — filtered means better, by default. Why do academic papers overwhelmingly use VLM cleaning? Under compute constraints the cascade degenerates to one layer, and if you keep only one, you keep the most versatile. Why did HunyuanVideo RL-train a captioner instead of using stock Qwen? Because captioning is the layer closest to the top that can still be aligned cheaply. Why does everyone treat the data flywheel as the endgame? Because it turns the top of the tower into a routine operation.

It also means "good data" has no attribute-style definition, only a stage-relative one: the same clip is good data at 256p pre-training and bad data at 768p fine-tuning. "Good" is not a property of the video; it is a relation among the video, the training stage, and the objective.

Further thoughts

Finally, a prediction that future facts are welcome to falsify. Our measured steady throughput was about 35–40 items/min/card for the 7B and about 30 for a 30B four times larger; per-card throughput barely moved, so serving efficiency is improving on a real schedule. If VLM inference cost falls another two orders of magnitude, the instrument argument weakens, and a migration of the filtering stage to VLM judges becomes the likely outcome — AVVA-style work and VLM reward models in RL are the early signs. Looked back on from there, today's CLIP+MLP aesthetic regressors will probably seem as primitive as pixel-threshold filtering does now.

The two pipelines are converging from opposite directions: we are learning to use VLMs as encoders, and frontier labs are replacing hand filters with learned judges. As for whether "good data" will finally get an epistemic definition once the cost truly collapses, or merely a more expensive consensus — I haven't figured that out yet.

Summary

With one bill of our own and six technical reports, this post took the industry's data pipeline apart into six layers and answered one question: why is the filtering stage VLM-free. The immediate reason is cost; the more essential one is identity — in this pipeline the VLM always works as an amplifier of human judgment, while a filter must be a stable instrument. They are not the same kind of thing.
