技术报告漫读：工业界怎么洗数据

在学界论文中，洗数据除了规格筛选外常常全程 VLM：对于 5B~14B 的小模型与十秒内的 480P 视频，只需要十几张卡较短时间内就能跑出一个差强人意的数据集。

最近尝试一遍洗数据 pipe，去重、初筛、重筛、富矿挖掘、事件窗定位，到 caption 重写与泄漏校验，多道工序除了第一道 ffprobe，其余全部由 VLM 严格出判决，做 SFT ablation 的效果仍然很不好。数据清洗本质上是个处理数据算力、数据丰富程度/质量等因素的 Tradeoff，工业界是如何洗出一份好的数据集的？

直接翻阅技术报告：MovieGen、Seedance 1.0、HunyuanVideo 1.5、Open-Sora 2.0，加上专门讲数据集的 Koala-36M 和 Tiger200K，新一点的还对照了 Seedance 2.0，精读一遍 Data 部分。首先管线里的确有 VLM——caption 和奖励模型。而过滤段从头到尾几乎没有用 VLM。

表 2｜六份技术报告的数据管线，按漏斗层级归纳。Seedance 2.0（2026）的数据引擎与 1.0 同构，未单列。

VLM 只出现在 caption、后训练的小规模判断，以及 RL 阶段的奖励模型。captioner 力求贴近人类写描述的方式，奖励模型放大人类的偏好判断。而在过滤段，也即镜头切分、质量过滤、运动过滤、去重这一段，清一色是 PySceneDetect、PaddleOCR、Laplacian 方差、VMAF、光流、CLIP+MLP 这些工具和小模型。

关于开源状态，Seedance 2.0 无权重，MiniMax H3 给权重不给报告，HunyuanVideo 报告权重都给了——但把数据处理管线这一真正的核心科技开源出来的一家大厂都没有。开源世界能找到的完整管线全部来自开源阵营：Open-Sora 把报告里的管线原样放进 repo，NeMo Curator 和 Data-Juicer 提供现成的过滤去重框架，再配上开源的 captioner、美学回归器和 Koala-36M、Tiger200K 这样的数据集，社区是拿开源工具复现工业 pipe。

第二层是一批便宜的量具。水印和字幕交给 PaddleOCR 和专用检测器，裁出安全区，裁完保留面积不足就整条丢弃；清晰度用 Laplacian 方差和 VMAF；美学是 CLIP+MLP 小回归器；运动用光流和 VMAF motion，太静和太抖都删。全是冻结的小模型，毫秒级一条。2026 年的 Seedance 2.0 报告里，这一层还是同样的配置。

第三层是去重与配比，操作对象从单条视频换成了分布。clip 用视频表征模型编码，聚类之后每个近重复簇只留质量分最高的那条；再按主体、场景、动作、风格数分布，头部下采样、尾部定向补采（Seedance），MovieGen 则用聚类反比采样加一套 600 个动词的词表做概念平衡。

第四层 caption，VLM 在这里进场，但没有一家裸用通用 VLM：Seedance 在 Tarsier2 上专训了双语 captioner，MovieGen 微调 LLaMA3-Video，HunyuanVideo 用 OPA-DPO 后训练来平衡描述密度与幻觉，输出的都是 80 到 200 词的密集结构化描述；Seedance 甚至单独训了一个 prompt 改写器，保证用户输入和 caption 同分布。

第五层是数据金字塔。同一批语料按质量分出梯度：Open-Sora 2.0 是 7000 万的低分辨率池喂出 500 万的高分辨率精选，HunyuanVideo 从 1000 万小时原始视频筛出 8 亿 clip，逐级收紧，贵的高分辨率训练只吃塔尖那一层。

后训练和 RLHF 不再是过滤大池子，而是人工构造小数据集。prompt 池人工验证，候选由不同 checkpoint 对同一 prompt 滚出，让人来做成对标注（HunyuanVideo 标了约 1 万对 DPO），最后把这 1 万对人类偏好蒸馏成 VLM 奖励模型供在线 RL 批量打分。

拼漏斗

每一层都有开源下位替代物。切分用 PySceneDetect 一行，转场敏感的场景换学习式的 TransNetV2；清晰度是 Laplacian 方差，运动是相邻帧光流的平均幅值，如：

from scenedetect import detect, ContentDetector
import cv2, numpy as np

scenes = detect("raw.mp4", ContentDetector(threshold=27))

def motion_sharpness(path):
    cap, prev, mags, laps = cv2.VideoCapture(path), None, [], []
    while True:
        ok, frame = cap.read()
        if not ok: break
        g = cv2.cvtColor(cv2.resize(frame, (640, 360)), cv2.COLOR_BGR2GRAY)
        laps.append(cv2.Laplacian(g, cv2.CV_64F).var())
        if prev is not None:
            flow = cv2.calcOpticalFlowFarneback(prev, g, None,
                    0.5, 3, 15, 3, 5, 1.2, 0)
            mags.append(np.linalg.norm(flow, axis=-1).mean())
        prev = g
    return np.median(laps), np.mean(mags)   # 清晰度、运动幅度
Qwen2.5-VL 或 Tarsier2 按六要素写 prompt——工业漏斗的每一层都有开源件，区别只在阈值要自己调、专训的 operator 没有现成的，这恰恰是最花时间的部分。

既然 VLM 可以靠近人类感官，为什么不在全流程尽量多用？

最直接当然是贵。六份报告没有一个披露数据处理本身的算力开销：HunyuanVideo 只给了「1000 万小时原始视频 → 8 亿 clip」的比例，Open-Sora 只给了 20 万美元的模型训练成本。

若设每条 6 卡秒，8 亿 clip，约 135 万 A800 小时，折合 150 卡年，即约一千张 A800 跑五十多天才筛一遍；而数据引擎常常每个模型版本迭代或表现不达预期都需要重筛。另一边，CLIP+MLP 美学回归器是毫秒级一帧。

进一步地，过滤器和 captioner 不一样：captioner 的输出直接进训练，好坏由下游模型消化；过滤器定义的是整个语料的分布，所以它首先得是一件量具，量具首先需要稳定。

CLIP+MLP 美学回归器是完全冻结的：同一个模型同一个分数语义，跑十遍结果一致，分数分布可以画出来审计。VLM 则是生成器：prompt 换个措辞、甚至同一批数据重跑一遍，分数的含义都可能漂移。

对于一个模型，什么是好数据无法量化，唯一的测法是拿去训练再评测，烧掉无法计量的卡时，最后得到数据配比 recipe。所以在工业界，加组件、加约束提升几个点的算法也许根本没那么重要。

Methods to get good data is all we need.

Tech Report Digest: How the Industry Cleans Data

In academic papers, data cleaning beyond basic metadata screening is often done end-to-end with VLMs: for 5B~14B models and sub-10-second 480p videos, a dozen GPUs for a short while is enough to produce a passable dataset.

Recently we ran a full data-cleaning pipeline — deduplication, first pass, re-screening, dense mining, event-window localization, caption rewriting, leakage checks; of the many stages, all but the first ffprobe pass were judged strictly by VLMs, and even so the SFT ablation results were still poor. That naturally suggests data cleaning is at bottom a trade-off among processing compute, data richness/quality, and similar factors. So how does the industry wash out a good dataset?

So we went straight to the technical reports: MovieGen, Seedance 1.0, HunyuanVideo 1.5, Open-Sora 2.0, plus Koala-36M and Tiger200K, the two dataset papers — and for the newer generation we also read through the Data section of Seedance 2.0. First, these pipelines certainly do use VLMs — captioning and reward models. What they almost never do is use a VLM anywhere in the filtering stage.

Before going further, here is what our small experiment cost; every extrapolation later takes it as the reference:

The cheapest stage is beyond doubt ffprobe: on CPU, it cut 152k down to 104k at essentially zero cost. Industry pipelines likewise start with exactly this kind of rough metadata screening.

Table 2. Data pipelines of the six technical reports, organized by funnel layer. The Seedance 2.0 (2026) data engine is isomorphic to 1.0 and not listed separately.

VLMs appear only in captioning, small-scale post-training judgments, and reward models in RL. The captioner strives to imitate how humans describe videos, and the reward model amplifies human preference. In the filtering stage — shot segmentation, quality filtering, motion filtering, dedup — it is PySceneDetect, PaddleOCR, Laplacian variance, VMAF, optical flow, and CLIP+MLP all the way down.

On open-source status: Seedance 2.0 gives no weights; MiniMax H3 gives weights but no report; HunyuanVideo gives both — yet the data processing pipeline, the real core technology, has been open-sourced by not a single major lab. The complete pipelines you can find in the open world all come from the open-source camp itself: Open-Sora put the exact pipeline from its report into the repo, NeMo Curator and Data-Juicer ship ready-made filtering and dedup frameworks, and together with open captioners, aesthetic regressors, and datasets like Koala-36M and Tiger200K, the community is reassembling the industrial pipeline with open tools.

The second layer is a batch of cheap instruments. Watermarks and subtitles go to PaddleOCR and dedicated detectors that crop a safe zone, discarding the clip if too little frame remains; sharpness is Laplacian variance and VMAF; aesthetics is a small CLIP+MLP regressor; motion is optical flow and VMAF motion, with both the too-still and the too-shaky removed. All frozen small models, milliseconds per item. In the 2026 Seedance 2.0 report, this layer runs the same configuration.

The third layer is dedup and rebalancing, where the object of operation shifts from individual clips to distributions. Clips are embedded by a video representation model and clustered; each near-duplicate cluster keeps only its highest-quality member. Then the distribution is counted over subject, scene, action, and style — heads are downsampled, tails get targeted acquisition (Seedance), and MovieGen combines inverse-proportional cluster sampling with a taxonomy of 600 human verbs for concept balancing.

The fourth layer is captioning, where VLMs enter — but none uses a stock general VLM: Seedance trained a bilingual captioner on Tarsier2, MovieGen fine-tuned LLaMA3-Video, HunyuanVideo used OPA-DPO post-training to balance description density against hallucination; the outputs are dense structured descriptions of 80 to 200 words. Seedance even trained a separate prompt rewriter so that user inputs stay in the same distribution as captions.

The fifth layer is the data pyramid. The same corpus is graded by quality: Open-Sora 2.0 feeds a 70M low-resolution pool down into a 5M high-resolution selection, and HunyuanVideo filters 10M raw hours into 800M clips, tightening level by level; expensive high-resolution training only eats from the top.

Post-training and RLHF no longer filter a big corpus; they hand-construct a small dataset. The prompt pool is human-verified, candidates are rolled out from different checkpoints on the same prompt, and humans label pairwise preferences (HunyuanVideo annotated about 10k DPO pairs), which are then distilled into a VLM reward model for bulk scoring during online RL.

Assemble the funnel

Every layer has an open-source, downgraded replacement. Segmentation is one line with PySceneDetect — switch to the learned TransNetV2 where cross-dissolves matter; sharpness is Laplacian variance, and motion is the mean magnitude of inter-frame optical flow, for example:

from scenedetect import detect, ContentDetector
import cv2, numpy as np

scenes = detect("raw.mp4", ContentDetector(threshold=27))

def motion_sharpness(path):
    cap, prev, mags, laps = cv2.VideoCapture(path), None, [], []
    while True:
        ok, frame = cap.read()
        if not ok: break
        g = cv2.cvtColor(cv2.resize(frame, (640, 360)), cv2.COLOR_BGR2GRAY)

                    0.5, 3, 15, 3, 5, 1.2, 0)

        prev = g

— Qwen2.5-VL or Tarsier2 with a six-element prompt — every layer of the industrial funnel has an open part; the difference is that thresholds need your own tuning, and the trained operators have no off-the-shelf equivalents, which is precisely the time-consuming part.

Since VLMs sit so close to human senses, why not use them as much as possible across the whole pipeline?

The most direct reason is cost. None of the six reports discloses the compute cost of data processing itself: HunyuanVideo gives only the ratio “10M raw hours → 800M clips”, Open-Sora only a $200k training budget.

Suppose six card-seconds per item; 800M clips comes to about 1.35M A800 hours, or 150 card-years — roughly a thousand A800s grinding for over fifty days for a single pass; and data engines routinely re-screen on every model iteration, or whenever results disappoint. On the other side, a CLIP+MLP aesthetic regressor runs at milliseconds per frame.

To go further: a filter is not a captioner. A captioner’s output goes straight into training and is digested by the downstream model; a filter defines the distribution of the entire corpus, so it is first of all a measuring instrument — and an instrument first needs to be stable.

A CLIP+MLP aesthetic regressor is frozen: same model, same score semantics, identical results across ten runs, known failure modes, and a score distribution you can plot and audit. A VLM is a generator: reword the prompt, upgrade the base model, or simply re-run the same batch, and the meaning of the score can drift. The same video scoring 4.2 this month and 4.8 next month is harmless for a caption and disastrous for a filter, because the selection criterion of the whole corpus just moved.

For a given model, what counts as good data cannot be quantified — the only way to measure it is to actually train and evaluate, burning uncountable GPU hours, and what you end up with is a data recipe. So in industry, algorithms that add components and constraints for a couple of benchmark points may simply not matter that much.

Methods to get good data is all we need.
