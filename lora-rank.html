给定基础权重，LoRA 的 r 怎么定

秩与奇异值谱

一个 \(d\times k\) 的矩阵有 \(\min(d,k)\) 个奇异值。秩等于非零奇异值的个数，只回答有几个非零方向的问题。

以 \(d=k=5120\) 的层为例，\(\Delta W\) 有 5120 个奇异值。全参微调之后通常是满秩的：5120 个奇异值全部非零，只是大多数极小。所以在这个尺度上问秩是多少得不到任何信息，有信息的是衰减速度。

式 1\[\begin{aligned}\Delta W&=W_{\mathrm{ft}}-W_0=U\Sigma V^{\top},\\ \Sigma&=\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_{\min(d,k)}),\\ &\quad \sigma_1\ge\sigma_2\ge\cdots\ge\sigma_{\min(d,k)}\ge 0,\\ \operatorname{rank}(\Delta W)&=\bigl|\{\,i:\sigma_i>0\,\}\bigr|.\end{aligned}\]

这里 \(U\) 和 \(V\) 的列分别是左右奇异向量，\(\sigma_i\) 是奇异值。秩只看 \(\sigma_i\) 是否为零，这正是它在微调场景里失效的原因。

LoRA 的秩上界

LoRA 把这层改动限制在一个低秩乘积上，见式 2。

式 2\[W=W_0+\frac{\alpha}{r}BA,\qquad B\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times r},\quad A\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times k},\quad r\ll\min(d,k).\]

初始化通常取 \(A\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)\)、\(B=0\)，于是 \(\Delta W=0\)，训练从原模型出发，起点没有扰动。约束来自一行代数，见式 3。

式 3\[\operatorname{rank}(\Delta W)=\operatorname{rank}(BA)\le\min(\operatorname{rank}A,\operatorname{rank}B)\le r.\]

r 设小了，最优解落在可达集合之外，怎么训都训不到。但训不到并不等于差，只要被丢掉的那些方向对 loss 贡献极小就无所谓。

截断 SVD 与能量占比

由 Eckart–Young 定理，截断 SVD 是最优的秩不超过 r 的近似，无论用 Frobenius 范数还是谱范数衡量，见式 4。

式 4\[\begin{aligned}\|\Delta W-\Delta W_r\|_F&=\min_{\operatorname{rank}(M)\le r}\|\Delta W-M\|_F=\Bigl(\sum_{i>r}\sigma_i^2\Bigr)^{1/2},\\ \|\Delta W-\Delta W_r\|_2&=\sigma_{r+1}.\end{aligned}\]

于是前 r 项的能量占比，见式 5。

式 5\[E(r)=\frac{\sum_{i\le r}\sigma_i^2}{\sum_{i=1}^{\min(d,k)}\sigma_i^2}=1-\frac{\|\Delta W-\Delta W_r\|_F^2}{\|\Delta W\|_F^2}\]

它衡量“秩 r 能保留多少改动”，判据因此具体。

有全参 ckpt 时的直接测量

逐层算 \(\Delta W=W_{\mathrm{ft}}-W_0\)，做 SVD，得到奇异值谱 \(\sigma_1\ge\dots\ge\sigma_{\min(d,k)}\ge 0\)，画 \(E(r)\) 的整条曲线，不要只看一个 r 点。

读谱得到的答案通常很大。根据 Biderman et al. 2024 在代码、数学任务上的测量，4096×4096 的 \(W_q\) 要 1500 以上的秩才解释 \(\Delta W\) 90% 的方差，比常用 LoRA 秩大一两个数量级；数据越多秩越高，MLP 比 attention 高，首尾层比中间层低。Shuttleworth et al. 2024 测到的全参更新有效秩也在 400 以上。所以谱上一般没有能取 r 的拐点：\(\Delta W\) 本身不小秩，LoRA 也不是靠复现它起作用的——Biderman et al. 2024 报告，代码、数学这类任务上 LoRA 追不上全参，提高 r 也补不平。

如果非要用“低秩逼近 \(\Delta W\)”这个标准，就按可接受的相对误差反推 r：Eckart–Young 给出 \(\|\Delta W-\Delta W_r\|_F/\|\Delta W\|_F=\sqrt{1-E(r)}\)（式 4、式 5），要 5% 以内就取满足它的最小 r。这么算出来的 r 通常大得没法用，这本身就是结论。

\(E(r)\) 高只说明全参解可以被低秩逼近，不说明 LoRA 找得到它：根据 Shuttleworth et al. 2024，低秩下 LoRA 学出的更新与全参谱不同，会出现与预训练谱近似正交的新方向，有效秩不到全参的一半。\(E(r)\) 也只数能量、不数重要性——谱上大的方向对任务可以并不重要，真实的 loss 增量由曲率决定，二阶近似下是 \(\tfrac12\sum_{i>r}h_i\sigma_i^2\)。所以最后仍要扫几个 r 跑 held-out，逐层分别定，不要只看全模型平均。

内在维度与早期谱代理

无全参 ckpt 时

一种是内在维度法。把更新限制在一个随机 d 维子空间里训至收敛，画 loss–d 曲线找拐点，见式 6。

式 6\[\theta=\theta_0+P\theta',\qquad P\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times d},\quad d\ll D,\qquad d^{*}=\min\bigl\{\,d:\ R(d)\ge 0.9\,R_{\mathrm{full}}\,\bigr\},\]

其中 \(R\) 是验证集指标，\(R_{\mathrm{full}}\) 是全参微调的结果。对应到 LoRA，有一个简单得多的做法：固定随机初始化的 \(A\)、只训 \(B\)。\(\Delta W=BA\) 的行空间被限制在 \(A\) 的 r 维行空间内，可训练参数量 \(d\cdot r\)。根据 Zhu et al. 2024，只训 B 比只训 A 有效，随机不训练的 A 和训过的 A 差不多；FLoRA（Hao et al. 2024）把这个更新近似成对梯度做一次随机投影。成本中等。

另一种是早期谱代理，成本低。全参先跑 200 到 500 步，对累计更新做 SVD，见式 7。

式 7\[S_T=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\Delta W_t,\qquad \Delta W_t=W_t-W_{t-1},\]

再对 \(S_T\) 套用式 5 计算 \(E(r)\)。以 SGD 为例 \(\Delta W_t=-\eta_t g_t\)，用 Adam 一类优化器时换成对应的更新量即可。这条路的先例都是拿这个谱去做初始化，不是用它定 r——SLoRA（Babakniya et al. 2023）用一段全参后的 \(\Delta W\) 初始化 LoRA，EVA（Paischer et al. 2024）用激活的增量 SVD。「主方向早期就成形、之后不再变」这个前提没有直接证据，反证倒有：GaLore（Zhao et al. 2024）默认每 200 步重取一次子空间。

跑这几百步比起跑完，代价🉑以忽略；但拿到的谱只能当粗估。

模块选择、α 缩放与逐层 r

同等参数量下，“全部线性层配小 r”通常优于“只用 q、v 配大 r”，优先覆盖 q、k、v、o 与 FFN。参数量相同不代表表达能力相同：前者可选的更新方向更多，只是每个方向更窄。

α 的缩放。真正生效的是 \(\alpha/r\)，见式 8。

式 8\[s=\frac{\alpha}{r},\qquad \Delta W=s\,BA.\]

常取 \(\alpha=2r\)，使有效尺度 \(s\) 恒为 2。改 r 必须同步改 α，否则等于偷偷改了学习率——把 r 从 \(r_1\) 换到 \(r_2\) 而 α 不变，更新整体乘上 \(r_1/r_2\)。若 r 很大，\(\alpha/r\) 会偏小，可考虑 \(\alpha/\sqrt{r}\) 一类的稳定化缩放。

逐层 r。\(E(r)\) 要按层分别算。根据 Biderman et al. 2024，首尾层的秩偏低、中间层偏高，MLP 比 attention 高；但逐层配置带来的收益往往不抵复杂度。

训后的参与比校验

对训好的 \(BA\) 做 SVD，算参与比，见式 9。

式 9\[\mathrm{PR}=\frac{\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sigma_i\bigr)^2}{\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sigma_i^2}\in[1,r].\]

奇异值分布均匀时它约等于 r，集中在单一方向时趋近 1。参与比远小于 r，说明 r 给多了，可以缩；奇异值尾部仍然很大，说明 r 卡住了该加。这一步能把冗余直接换成推理上的节省：权重合并进 \(W\) 后推理没有额外成本，但在多套 adapter 并存、不合并权重时，开销与 r 成正比。

边界

\(E(r)\ge 0.9975\)（即 5% 相对误差，由式 4、式 5 反推）说明改动方向被低秩捕获。式 4 逼近的是 \(\Delta W\) 的 Frobenius 误差，训练优化的是 loss，两者只有在 loss 对 \(\Delta W\) 各方向的敏感度相同时才一致。所以这是与全参差距小的必要条件代理，而非充分条件：截断 SVD 是最优近似，不代表训练找得到它，loss 相当也不代表解相同。最终仍要靠 held-out 验证。

此外，所需的秩取决于数据量。数据越多，最优 \(\Delta W\) 越可能带上高秩分量，同一任务样本从两万增到两百万，所需 r 会变大；任务离预训练分布越远，需要的维度越高。

How to Choose LoRA's Rank r Given a Base Checkpoint

Rank and the singular-value spectrum

A \(d\times k\) matrix has \(\min(d,k)\) singular values. The rank equals the number of nonzero singular values, and it answers exactly one question: how many nonzero directions are there.

Take a layer with \(d=k=5120\). Then \(\Delta W\) has 5120 singular values. After full fine-tuning it is usually full rank: all 5120 are nonzero, but most of them are tiny. Asking "what is the rank" at this scale carries no information. The decay rate does.

Equation 1\[\begin{aligned}\Delta W&=W_{\mathrm{ft}}-W_0=U\Sigma V^{\top},\\ \Sigma&=\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_{\min(d,k)}),\\ &\quad \sigma_1\ge\sigma_2\ge\cdots\ge\sigma_{\min(d,k)}\ge 0,\\ \operatorname{rank}(\Delta W)&=\bigl|\{\,i:\sigma_i>0\,\}\bigr|.\end{aligned}\]

Here the columns of \(U\) and \(V\) are the left and right singular vectors, and \(\sigma_i\) are the singular values. Rank only looks at whether each \(\sigma_i\) is zero. That is precisely why it fails as a tool in this setting.

The rank cap in LoRA

LoRA restricts the update to a low-rank product:

Equation 2\[W=W_0+\frac{\alpha}{r}BA,\qquad B\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times r},\quad A\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times k},\quad r\ll\min(d,k).\]

Initialization usually takes \(A\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)\) and \(B=0\), so \(\Delta W=0\) and training starts from the pretrained function with no perturbation. The constraint then follows from one line of algebra:

Equation 3\[\operatorname{rank}(\Delta W)=\operatorname{rank}(BA)\le\min(\operatorname{rank}A,\operatorname{rank}B)\le r.\]

If r is too small, the optimum lies outside the reachable set, and no amount of training gets there. But not getting there is not the same as being worse: as long as the discarded directions contribute very little to the loss, it does not matter.

Truncated SVD and the energy ratio

By the Eckart–Young theorem, the truncated SVD is the optimal approximation of rank at most r in both the Frobenius and the spectral norm:

Equation 4\[\begin{aligned}\|\Delta W-\Delta W_r\|_F&=\min_{\operatorname{rank}(M)\le r}\|\Delta W-M\|_F=\Bigl(\sum_{i>r}\sigma_i^2\Bigr)^{1/2},\\ \|\Delta W-\Delta W_r\|_2&=\sigma_{r+1}.\end{aligned}\]

The retained energy fraction is therefore

Equation 5\[E(r)=\frac{\sum_{i\le r}\sigma_i^2}{\sum_{i=1}^{\min(d,k)}\sigma_i^2}=1-\frac{\|\Delta W-\Delta W_r\|_F^2}{\|\Delta W\|_F^2}\]

which measures how much of the change rank r preserves; that makes the decision rule concrete.

Measuring directly with a full checkpoint

Compute \(\Delta W=W_{\mathrm{ft}}-W_0\) layer by layer, take the SVD to get \(\sigma_1\ge\dots\ge\sigma_{\min(d,k)}\ge 0\), and plot the whole \(E(r)\) curve rather than a single rank.

The answer the spectrum gives is usually large. Per Biderman et al. 2024, measured on code and math tasks, a \(4096\times4096\) \(W_q\) needs a rank above 1500 to explain 90% of the variance in \(\Delta W\), one to two orders of magnitude above typical LoRA ranks; rank grows with more data, MLP modules rank above attention, and first and last layers rank below middle ones. Shuttleworth et al. 2024 measure effective ranks above 400 for full fine-tuning updates. So the curve usually offers no knee to read an r off: \(\Delta W\) is not low rank, and LoRA does not work by reproducing it — Biderman et al. 2024 report that on code and math, LoRA trails full fine-tuning and raising r does not close the gap.

If you insist on the "low-rank approximation of \(\Delta W\)" standard, fix the relative error you can accept and invert it: Eckart–Young gives \(\|\Delta W-\Delta W_r\|_F/\|\Delta W\|_F=\sqrt{1-E(r)}\) (Equation 4, Equation 5), so a 5% budget means the smallest r that satisfies it. The r that comes out is usually far too large to use, which is itself the conclusion.

A high \(E(r)\) only says the full-fine-tuning solution can be approximated at low rank, not that LoRA finds it: per Shuttleworth et al. 2024, at low rank LoRA learns an update whose spectrum differs from full fine-tuning, with new directions nearly orthogonal to the pretrained spectrum and an effective rank under half of full fine-tuning's. \(E(r)\) also counts energy, not importance — a direction that is large in the spectrum can matter little for the task, and the real change in loss is set by curvature, \(\tfrac12\sum_{i>r}h_i\sigma_i^2\) to second order. So still sweep a few ranks against held-out data, per layer rather than as a model-wide average.

Intrinsic dimension and the early spectral proxy

Without a full checkpoint

The first is the intrinsic-dimension method. Restrict the update to a random d-dimensional subspace, train to convergence, and look for the knee of the loss–d curve:

Equation 6\[\theta=\theta_0+P\theta',\qquad P\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times d},\quad d\ll D,\qquad d^{*}=\min\bigl\{\,d:\ R(d)\ge 0.9\,R_{\mathrm{full}}\,\bigr\},\]

where \(R\) is a validation metric and \(R_{\mathrm{full}}\) is the full fine-tuning result. Translated back to LoRA, it reduces to something much cheaper: freeze a randomly initialized \(A\) and train only \(B\). The row space of \(\Delta W=BA\) is confined to the \(r\)-dimensional row space of \(A\), with \(d\cdot r\) trainable parameters. Per Zhu et al. 2024, training B alone is more effective than training A alone, and a random untrained A performs nearly as well as a fine-tuned one; FLoRA (Hao et al. 2024) approximates this update as a random projection of the gradient. Moderate cost.

The second is the early spectral proxy, and it is cheap. Run full fine-tuning for 200–500 steps and take the SVD of the accumulated update:

Equation 7\[S_T=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\Delta W_t,\qquad \Delta W_t=W_t-W_{t-1},\]

then apply Equation 5 to \(S_T\) to get \(E(r)\). Under SGD, \(\Delta W_t=-\eta_t g_t\); with Adam-style optimizers, substitute the corresponding update. The precedents for this route all use the spectrum for initialization rather than for choosing r — SLoRA (Babakniya et al. 2023) initializes LoRA from the SVD of a short full fine-tuning run, and EVA (Paischer et al. 2024) uses an incremental SVD of activations. The premise that the dominant directions form early and then stop moving has no direct support, and there is counter-evidence: GaLore (Zhao et al. 2024) re-extracts its subspace every 200 steps by default.

A few hundred steps are negligible next to a full run, but the spectrum they give you is only a rough estimate.

Target modules, α scaling, and per-layer rank

At equal parameter budget, "all linear layers with a small r" usually beats "q and v only with a large r". Cover q, k, v, o and the FFN layers first. Equal parameter count is not equal expressive capacity: the broader configuration has more directions available, each a narrower one.

The α scaling. What actually takes effect is \(\alpha/r\):

Equation 8\[s=\frac{\alpha}{r},\qquad \Delta W=s\,BA.\]

The common choice \(\alpha=2r\) holds the effective scale \(s\) at 2. If you change r you must change α with it, or you have quietly changed the learning rate: moving from \(r_1\) to \(r_2\) with α fixed multiplies the whole update by \(r_1/r_2\). For very large r, \(\alpha/r\) becomes small, and a stabilization such as \(\alpha/\sqrt{r}\) is worth considering.

Per-layer rank. Read \(E(r)\) for each layer separately. Per Biderman et al. 2024, first and last layers rank lower and middle layers higher, and MLP modules rank above attention; but per-layer configuration rarely repays its complexity.

Checking the trained adapter

Take the SVD of the trained \(BA\) and compute the participation ratio:

Equation 9\[\mathrm{PR}=\frac{\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sigma_i\bigr)^2}{\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sigma_i^2}\in[1,r].\]

It is approximately r when the singular values are spread evenly, and approaches 1 when they concentrate in a single direction. A participation ratio well below r means r was set too high and can be reduced; a heavy tail means r is binding and should be raised. This step turns redundancy directly into inference savings: merging the adapter into \(W\) removes inference cost entirely, but when several adapters must be served unmerged, cost scales with r.

Limits

\(E(r)\ge 0.9975\) — a 5% relative error, inverted from Equation 4 and Equation 5 — says that the update is captured by a low-rank subspace. Equation 4 approximates the Frobenius error of \(\Delta W\), while training minimizes a loss; the two agree only if the loss is equally sensitive to every direction of \(\Delta W\). So this is a necessary-condition proxy for being close to full fine-tuning, not a sufficient one: the truncated SVD is the optimal approximation, but that does not mean training finds it, and comparable loss does not mean the same solution. Held-out validation still decides.

The required rank also depends on data volume. More data makes high-rank components of the optimal \(\Delta W\) more likely: for the same task, going from twenty thousand samples to two million raises the r you need. Tasks further from the pretraining distribution need more dimensions as well.
